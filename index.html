About

I am Professor of Political Science at the University of Oslo, where I have been based since completing my PhD in 2015. My research sits at the intersection of comparative politics and international relations, with a focus on armed conflict, regime change, autocratic politics and mass mobilization. I use quantitative methods and large-scale historical datasets to study how political institutions shape conflict, how citizens respond to autocracy and democratic erosion, and what drives popular uprisings and protest.

I am Principal Investigator of the ERC Consolidator Grant project Authoritarian Threats to Scientific Knowledge (AutoKnow), which uses AI and large-scale text analysis to study how authoritarian rule affects science. I direct the Peace and Conflict Studies master’s programme at the University of Oslo and serve as Associate Editor of the Journal of Peace Research.

In 2024–2025 I was a Visiting Fellow at Nuffield College, University of Oxford, and in 2019 a Visiting Researcher at the Center for European Studies, Harvard University. I have previously held positions as Associate Professor at the University of Oslo and Senior Researcher at the Peace Research Institute Oslo (PRIO). I received the Fridtjof Nansen Award for Young Researchers in 2024 and the King’s Gold Medal for the best PhD dissertation at the Faculty of Social Sciences in 2016.

Projects

Principal Investigator · ERC Consolidator Grant · 2026–2031

Authoritarian Threats to Scientific Knowledge (AutoKnow)

How is science shaped by authoritarian rule? AutoKnow uses AI to analyse millions of research publications from 1950 to 2025, tracing political bias, censorship and lost novelty, and asks when regimes attack scientists and whether political pressure erodes core scientific norms.

Funded by the European Research Council · Project page →

Objections to Democracy (ODe)

Why do citizens accept or reject democratic norms? Survey and experimental evidence across democracies.

When Will Citizens Defend Democracy? (DefDem)

Experimental and observational research on citizens’ willingness to act against democratic backsliding.

Mobilizing for and Against Democracy (MoDe)

The conditions under which mass mobilization supports or undermines democratic governance.

Moral Beliefs about Violent Political Conflict (MoViCon)

How moral reasoning shapes public support for political violence, civil resistance and interstate war.

Policies of Dictatorships (PoD)

How autocratic regimes formulate and implement domestic policy, and how support coalitions shape their choices.

The Emergence, Life, and Demise of Autocratic Regimes (ELDAR)

Historical and comparative analysis of how autocracies form, persist and collapse.

Norwegian Democracy Assessment (TiNDe)

An ongoing assessment of the state of Norwegian democracy across institutional and attitudinal dimensions.
